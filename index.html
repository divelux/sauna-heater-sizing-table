Where the makers disagree: glass and stone

Each square meter (10.8 sq ft) of uninsulated surface adds this much to the top of the range:

RuleExtra volume per m²Extra kW per m² (per 10 sq ft)
Harvia (used in the tables above)1.2 m³1.2 kW (1.1 kW)
HUUM1 m³1.0 kW (0.9 kW)
"Sizing Your HUUM", North America1.5 m³ (4.92 cu ft per sq ft)1.5 kW (1.4 kW)

The dataset has all three rules side by side for every row. With no glass or stone they agree.

How the numbers are worked out

Every cell is the output of the open-source Sauna Heater Size Calculator (its heater.js is copied into this folder unchanged). No rule is added for this table, and none of the figures are ours.

  1. Calculated volume = room volume (× 1.5 for bare log walls) + 1.2 m³ for each m² of uninsulated glass, stone, tile, brick or concrete. Harvia.
  2. Top of the range: 1 kW per m³ of calculated volume. Harvia and HUUM both give this average.
  3. Bottom of the range: 1 kW per 50 cu ft (1.416 m³). A shortcut many North American retailers quote, and where Harvia's own worked example lands (about 8.5 m³, "a minimum output of 6 kW").
  4. Wood-burning stoves are sold by room volume, not kW. For a wood stove, look up the calculated volume in the CSV or JSON and shop for a stove rated for it.

What this table does not know. Insulation quality, vents, outdoor temperature, stone mass and your home's wiring all change the answer, which is why every cell is a range. Treat it as a shopping shortlist: check the room volume range printed for the exact model, and have a licensed electrician confirm the circuit before anything is installed.
